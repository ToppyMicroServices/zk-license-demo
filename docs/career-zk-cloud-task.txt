User objective

Build a small, executable research prototype that answers questions about a
person's career without disclosing the underlying career record. Investigate
whether proving that an answer is stable across an agreed finite set of
admissible interpretations offers a useful research contribution. Novelty is
unconfirmed. Do not claim a new cryptographic primitive or that general ZK
queries over career credentials are new.

First milestone

Use one structured question family: as of an agreed date, does this person have
at least N days of Python production-operations experience within a stated
time window? Project participation alone is not proof of operational duties.
Use only signed synthetic evidence. Arbitrary natural-language interpretation,
real resumes, JPKI integration, and external LLM/API services are outside this
milestone. Implement the prototype rather than stopping at a design document.

Proof relation and result semantics

1. Define a small authenticated record format, trusted issuers chosen by the
   verifier, subject consistency, evidence validity, and a precisely scoped
   completeness declaration. Do not interpret missing records as absence of
   experience. A completeness declaration is an issuer assertion within a
   scope, not proof of all facts about a person's real career.
2. Derive every admissible interpretation deterministically from the evidence
   and the verifier-agreed policy. Initially bound inputs to three records and
   eight interpretations. The prover cannot omit unfavorable interpretations
   or choose a weaker policy. Reject empty sets, invalid dates, unsupported
   inputs, and bounds overflow as errors.
3. Evaluate all interpretations. Merge overlapping qualifying intervals before
   counting days, and define date boundaries. YES means all interpretations
   satisfy the predicate. NO requires all to fail and certified complete
   coverage of the queried scope. Disagreement, or missing coverage that could
   change the answer, gives UNDETERMINED. Sufficient certified positive evidence
   can support YES without assuming that the rest of the career is complete.
   Invalid evidence, proof failure, and execution failure are errors, never NO
   or UNDETERMINED masquerading as successful verification.
4. Use genuine ZK proof generation and cryptographic verification. Bind evidence
   authenticity and subject consistency into the proved relation or an explicitly
   justified binding composition; an unconstrained host-side signature check is
   insufficient. Bind the canonical query, policy version, evaluation date,
   issuer configuration, evidence commitment, answer, and verification context.
   Bind any status snapshot, audience, or nonce used by the implementation.
   The verifier must check its expected statement, not accept weaker parameters
   supplied with the proof. Document all public inputs and possible correlation
   or guessing from commitments. Do not claim protection from every inference
   obtainable through repeated legitimate queries.
